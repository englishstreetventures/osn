## Open

- [x] **The RSVP sheet took too long to close after Save** (`claude/rsvp-modal-close-delay-d8sqnd`, 2026-08-08) — user-reported: "quite a delay for the form to close after you click save". The dwell was a flat `SAVED_DWELL_MS = 900`, started when the server's 200 landed, so what a guest actually waited through was **round-trip + 900ms** — the two costs stacked, and the slower the network the longer the sheet sat there having already finished its job. `POST /api/rsvp` is six sequential D1 round-trips behind a Worker — the session lookup in `sessionAuth`'s `derive` plus five in the handler (see `[[perf]]` P-W1), so half a second of "Saving…" followed by nine tenths of a second of "Saved" was the ordinary case, not the tail. The dwell is now a **budget measured from the click**: `savedDwellMs(requestMs)` returns `max(SAVED_DWELL_MIN_MS, SAVED_DWELL_MS - requestMs)`, `SAVED_DWELL_MS` drops 900 → 600 and becomes the *ceiling* rather than a fixed hold, and `SAVED_DWELL_MIN_MS = 500` is the floor. So the request time comes **out** of the dwell instead of being added to it. Be precise about how far that goes: up to the KNEE (a reply of `SAVED_DWELL_MS - SAVED_DWELL_MIN_MS`) the budget is spent exactly and click-to-close is flat at ~600ms; past it the floor takes over and the total is `request + 500`, growing with the network again. The floor is sized by the **spoken** confirmation rather than the visible one (C-L1 in `[[security]]`), which makes the knee small — so what most real saves get is the floor, i.e. **900 → 500 of dwell, a flat ~400ms off every save**, rather than the constant click-to-close the budget alone would give. The remaining ~200ms is recoverable, but only by decoupling the announcement (C-L1), not by lowering the floor. The floor is what keeps this from re-becoming the original bug (`[[changelog/completed-features]]`, "a recorded RSVP gave the guest no confirmation"): "Saving…" is not a confirmation, only the dwell shows "Saved", so a reply that outran the whole budget still gets a readable confirmed state. **The pre-PR review then resized that floor 300 → 500** — it turns out to be an accessibility constant, not a taste one: the sheet's `sr-only role="status"` region is destroyed on the same tick the dwell expires (the timer calls `props.onClose()` directly, bypassing `modalExit`, so there is no grace period) while focus returns to the Respond button, and the `solid-toast` hedge is weaker than it looks because each toast's live region is created *with* its content. Filed as **C-L1** in `[[security]]` with the proper fix (a page-root live region beside the `<Toaster>`), which would let the floor drop back. **Nothing downstream needed retuning**, and that is by design rather than luck — the Respond-button celebration is measured from the moment the sheet uncovers that button (`onConfirmed` fires *with* `onClose`, the fix from `claude/pr388-deploy-mobile-invite-jlz8in`), and the toast is already up and outlives the close, so the dwell is the only thing the number controls. Two guards keep `savedDwellMs` from ever exceeding its ceiling — a non-finite input returns the floor (`Math.max` loses to NaN, and a NaN `setTimeout` delay fires immediately, i.e. the sheet vanishes with no confirmation), and `requestMs` is clamped at zero (`Date.now()` is wall-clock and an NTP step backwards would otherwise *lengthen* the dwell). That ceiling property is load-bearing for the suite: ~20 existing tests land the close with `vi.advanceTimersByTimeAsync(SAVED_DWELL_MS)`, and they all keep passing untouched precisely because no input can make the dwell longer than that — `rsvp-saved.test.ts` now pins it explicitly alongside the budget arithmetic. `RsvpModal.test.tsx` gained two tests driving a *controllable* reply (a fetch stub whose promise the test resolves) rather than the instant-resolve mock every other test uses, since instant resolution is exactly the case that cannot tell the old behaviour from the new: one asserts a reply slower than the budget still gets the floor **and nothing beyond it**, the other that a 200ms reply closes 400ms later. Also swapped the clock to `performance.now()` (perf P-I1): this measures a DURATION, and a monotonic clock cannot step backwards the way wall-clock can under an NTP correction — verified the fake-timer suite is unaffected, since vitest fakes `performance` under jsdom. web 795 → 805 tests, including a two-sided timing assertion on the host-preview call site (T-M1 — it passes a literal `requestMs` of 0 and nothing constrained it; changing it to 10 000 kept all 803 green) and a knee test derived from the constants rather than their current values (T-M2). Eight stale timing comments quoting the old 900ms dwell — and three quoting a 1400ms choreography that has been 900ms since #397 — were corrected (T-U1). The browser tier's mid-dwell assertions were hardened rather than left to a 33%-narrower wall clock (T-E1): the three "sheet still up, nothing sweeping yet" checks now sample inside ONE `waitFor` predicate so they cannot straddle the dwell's expiry, and the two `InvitePage.browser.test.tsx` "Saved" anchors accept a closed sheet instead of throwing on `null` and retrying to their deadline. **Not changed:** the five D1 round-trips themselves — filed as `[[perf]]` P-W1 rather than fixed here, because two of the three parallelisable reads run *after* the ownership check on purpose and reordering them for latency would query `guest_events` for guest ids straight off an unvalidated body.

Drive-bys: escaped the code input's `pattern` hyphen (Chrome v-flag rejected the whole pattern), and `deploy.yml` now bakes `PUBLIC_OSN_ISSUER_URL` into the guest-site build (bundle was dialling `http://localhost:4000` for the Pulse account-link island in prod).
