# How a cire wedding with several owners receives its cash gifts — design

Date: 2026-11-17, revised three times on 2026-11-18 with the owner's answers · Status: every question answered by the owner; awaiting the owner's final read · Issue: englishstreetventures/osn#1950 (follows #1336; related to #1248 and #2018)

## Goal

A wedding can have several owners so that both partners hold owner rights (`2026-10-01-cire-multi-owner-design.md`, §Goal), but its cash gifts are paid into one Stripe account, which carries one person's identity and bank details. Today that person keeps the account when they stop being an owner, nobody else can replace it, and refunds or disputes on gifts paid into an account the wedding no longer names are lost to its gift log. This note says who may connect, change or replace the payout account; what happens to it when its holder would leave; how the couple hand it from one partner to the other; and what each owner sees. It replaces the stopgap the multi-owner design's decision 14 asked for, which #1336 built.

This note designs; it builds nothing. The owner approves it before any code.

## Decisions

The decisions are kept in the order the owner made them. Where a later answer replaced an earlier one, the earlier one stays, marked **superseded** with the decision that replaced it.

### Owner, 2026-11-17

1. **When the owner who connected the wedding's Stripe account stops being an owner** — removed, left, demoted, or their account erased — all three of these hold:
   - (a) money gifts close while the connector holds no owner seat. The same holds for a wedding whose connector is not recorded. The Money gifts panel says why in plain words.
   - (b) the remaining owners are told, in the owner-change notice and in the Money gifts panel, and pointed at the money-gifts switch.
   - (c) the connector can hand the account to another owner before leaving.

   This replaces two defaults the merged #1975 set: an account with no recorded connector belonged to no owner, and a connector who left kept the account. A third default stays: refreshing the account's status is open to every owner. *Since decision 26, removal, leaving and demotion are refused while the person holds the account, so of the four only erasure remains; (a) still covers it, an unrecorded connector, and any account whose holder left before this ships.*
2. **Cire never holds gift money.** Gifts stay direct charges on the couple's connected account (`wiki/cire/cire-registry.md`; the `registry_settings.stripe_account_id` comment, `cire/db/src/schema.ts:1016-1019`). A design that routes gifts through the platform account is out.

### Owner, 2026-11-18 (answers to the first version's questions)

3. **The design as a whole**: a hand-over is a new account and a retire, never a move.
4. **The gap during a hand-over is accepted**: gifts are closed from the retire until the next account can take payments.
5. **Any owner may replace an absent holder's account.**
6. **A retired Standard account stays connected for 120 days** before cire hands it back.
7. **The notice and the panel offer two actions** — connect your own account, or turn money gifts off — and the switch survives a change of account.
8. **The hour is accepted** in which a guest already holding a payment page can still pay into the old account.
9. **Erasure**: the panel tells the other owners, the deletion preview warns the holder, and an email goes to the remaining owners as well.
10. **Tier purchases are in scope**, tied to the account that paid. *Superseded by decision 25.*
11. **The holder of an Express account cire created gets one email when they stop being an owner.** *Superseded by decision 26: a holder can no longer leave, be removed or be demoted while holding the account, and an erased holder has no address left (decision 24), so no one is left to send it to.*
12. **Editors are not told** that the account's holder is not an owner.
13. **The departed holder is not told** when the remaining owners replace their account.
14. **At Stripe, not in code:** "Issue refunds" and "Manage disputes" stay on in the Express Dashboard settings (owner action, below).

### Owner, 2026-11-18 (answers to the revised version's questions)

15. **A departed holder's own Standard account stays on the old wedding** until another owner replaces it, and while it does, it cannot be connected through OAuth to another wedding of theirs (`accountMayJoin`, `registry.ts:1623-1630`, as today). Their exit is to revoke cire in their own Stripe Dashboard, which clears it from every wedding.
16. A tier hold of 14 days after the paying owner leaves. *Superseded by decision 25.*
17. Only the purchase behind the current tier ties it to a person. *Superseded by decision 25.*
18. The departing payer may ask for a refund of that purchase; later answered with a 30-day window and an answer on a refund after a higher tier bought on top. *All superseded by decision 25.*
19. The payer is the profile that started the checkout. *Superseded by decision 25.*
20. What "nothing is taken back" covers when a tier falls. *Superseded by decision 25.*
21. A payer who left before this ships starts no hold. *Superseded by decision 25.*
22. Only the current tier can be kept. *Superseded by decision 25.*
23. Owners see which co-owner paid. *Superseded by decision 25.*
24. **The erased person cannot be emailed**; the deletion preview carries the message instead (see behaviour 10).

### Owner, 2026-11-18 (simplification)

25. **The tier belongs to the wedding**, as it does today: a permanent grant, with no link to the owner who paid. Tier purchases are out of this design's scope again. Every payer-following feature of the previous version — the hold, its 14 days, the keep purchase, the `lapsed` status, the lowering sweep, the banner, refund on request and its page — is gone.
26. **The payout-account holder cannot leave, be removed or be demoted while they hold the wedding's payout account.** They must hand it over first: an owner replaces it, or the holder disconnects it and money gifts close. Erasure is the one way an owner can vanish while holding an account.

### Owner, 2026-11-18 (answer to the simplified version's question)

27. **Any owner may replace the payout account while its holder is still an owner**, by retiring it and connecting their own; the holder is told by the retire notice (behaviour 8). This is decision 26's "an owner replaces it" read as written. The owner chose it over letting only the holder start a hand-over, which would have left a holder who will not hand over impossible to remove or replace.

**Owner action at Stripe:** keep "Issue refunds" and "Manage disputes" turned on in the Express Dashboard settings, since after a retire the old holder is the one person who can refund or answer a dispute on their gifts there.

## Non-goals

- Tier purchases. The tier rests on the wedding, not on a person, so an owner leaving does not touch it (decision 25).
- A planner as the payout holder (#2018). This note only leaves room for it: [Planner (#2018)](#planner-2018).
- Two payout accounts on one wedding, or gifts split between partners.
- Any change to who may run a gift refund. Cire refunds no gift on its own today, and still will not.

## What Stripe allows

Checked against Stripe's documentation on 2026-11-17, each page fetched that day. Cire pins API version `2025-03-31.basil` (`cire/api/src/services/stripe.ts:37`).

**Cire cannot hand an Express account from one person to another.**

- "After you create an account link for an Express connected account, you can't read or update its KYC information." (docs.stripe.com/connect/express-accounts, §Create an account link). Cire creates the link on the first Connect press (`registry-stripe.ts:321`), so from then on cire cannot change whose identity the account carries.
- The holder edits their own personal details and payout bank in the Express Dashboard, and signs in there with the account's email and a code sent to their phone or email (docs.stripe.com/connect/express-dashboard, §Account settings, §Direct access). So the person who onboarded keeps control of the account whatever cire records.
- Stripe treats a change to a verified tax ID as a transfer of the account and its balances to a new owner, which the account must accept again under the Stripe Services Agreement (docs.stripe.com/connect/update-verified-information, Accounts v1, §Update the account tax identification number). That is a matter between the two people and Stripe, not a step cire can run.

So **a hand-over is a new account**: the next holder connects one of their own, and the wedding stops naming the old one. This note calls that **retiring** the old account.

**A Standard account** (connected through OAuth, `stripe_account_type = 'standard'`, `schema.ts:1040`) is the holder's own Stripe account. Who owns it inside Stripe is theirs to manage; cire treats it as it treats an Express account, except that cire later hands it back (behaviour 11).

**Retiring is not deleting or rejecting.**

- Deleting a live Express account needs every balance at zero (docs.stripe.com/api/accounts/delete), and a deleted account can no longer refund.
- Rejecting is for fraud or a terms breach, and turns off the account's charges and payouts (docs.stripe.com/api/account/reject). That would stop the old holder's own payouts.
- So a retired Express account stays connected to cire, untouched. Its holder still sees it, and still receives its payouts, in the Express Dashboard.

**Refunds and disputes on old gifts keep arriving while the account stays connected.** For direct charges, "the payment, refunds, and disputes exist on the connected account, so their events use the Connected accounts scope", and "each event for a connected account contains a top-level `account` property" (docs.stripe.com/connect/webhooks, Accounts v1). So cire's gift handlers must match them by the account the gift was paid into, not by the account the wedding names now. Once cire hands a Standard account back, its events stop reaching cire.

**Cire stays liable for a retired Express account's losses.** "Your platform is responsible for losses incurred by Express connected accounts" (docs.stripe.com/connect/express-accounts, §Before you begin). A dispute on an old gift debits the old account whether or not the wedding still names it. Retiring changes nothing here.

**Refunds on old gifts.** In the Express Dashboard an account holder can "issue refunds and manage disputes, if you enable these features" (docs.stripe.com/connect/express-dashboard, §Payments). After a retire, the old holder is the one person who can refund a gift paid into their account from that Dashboard.

**Idempotency keys last at least 24 hours.** Stripe saves the first answer to a key and replays it to a repeat, and may prune a key once it is 24 hours old; it also asks for no "personal identifiers" in keys (docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests). The account-create key is `cire-account-<weddingId>` (`stripe.ts:530`), so a Connect press within 24 hours of a retire would get the retired account back (the hazard noted on #1950 for deauthorization). The key must change with each account, and the attach must refuse an account made under an old key; see [Data model](#data-model).

**Not confirmed:** whether Stripe scopes an idempotency key to the connected account named in `Stripe-Account`. The design does not depend on it: the gift checkout key gains the account id (behaviour 7).

## Terms

- **Holder**: the profile in `stripe_connected_by_osn_profile_id` for the account the wedding names now. Today's code calls them the connector.
- **Seated**: the holder holds a seat with `role = 'owner'` on this wedding.
- **Absent**: there is an account and its holder is not seated, or none is recorded. After this ships, a holder becomes absent only by erasure; an unrecorded connector, a holder who left before this ships, or a row written by hand are the other ways a wedding can be in this state.
- **Retire**: the wedding stops naming its account. Cire calls Stripe for nothing when it retires one.

## Who may connect, change or replace the account

| Action | Who | Today |
|---|---|---|
| Connect a wedding with no account (a new Express account, a reuse from another wedding, or OAuth) | any owner | unchanged |
| Continue onboarding | the holder, while seated | unchanged |
| Open the Stripe Dashboard (Standard) | shown to the holder; it is a plain link, and Stripe's own sign-in guards it | unchanged |
| Check again (refresh the status) | any owner | unchanged (the #1975 default that stays) |
| **Hand over**: retire the account and name the owner who should connect the next | the holder, while seated | new |
| **Disconnect**: retire the account, naming no one | the holder, while seated | new |
| **Replace**: retire the account, then connect as above | any owner — when the holder is absent (decision 5), and while the holder is seated (decision 27) | new |
| Leave, be removed or be demoted | refused for the holder while they hold the account (decision 26, behaviour 9) | new |
| Anything above | editors and viewers: nothing | unchanged |

A replace by another owner while the holder is seated mails every owner, the holder included (behaviour 8), so it is never silent. Naming an owner in a hand-over grants them nothing the others lack: once the account is retired any owner may connect the next one, as for any wedding with no account. The name tells the notice whom to address.

The Stripe routes sit behind `weddingTier(db, "gold")` (`registry-stripe.ts:220`), and stay there. The retire does not: it calls Stripe for nothing and only clears cire's own row, so it is mounted behind `weddingOwner` and the per-user limiter alone. A holder on a wedding an operator lowered below Gold (`grant-tier.ts --lower`, the one way a wedding with an account falls below Gold) can then still hand over, so the refusal of behaviour 9 holds on every tier, as decision 26 says.

Whether the holder is seated is never stored. Every place that builds a settings record works it out with one `EXISTS` on `wedding_hosts`, answered by the unique index `wedding_hosts_wedding_profile_uniq` (`schema.ts:209`): `settingsOnly`, `settingsWithCurrency`, the guest gate's read, `updateSettings`'s `RETURNING` (`registry.ts:2029-2034`), `writeStripeAccountState`'s (`:1116-1121`), the attach's (`:2229-2232`), the reuse's and its refusal read (`:2384-2389`, `:2398-2422`), and `defaultSettings` (no account, so never absent). One missed and the refresh, the panel and the gate disagree.

**The account-create key** becomes `cire-account-<weddingId>-<generation>`, and `createAccount` takes the generation (`stripe.ts:507`). Two owners who press Connect at once still get one account (the race the session route handles at `registry-stripe.ts:288-301`), and the key holds no personal identifier. An account created under the old key format in the 24 hours before the deploy and never stored is orphaned, as such an account is today after 24 hours.

**The attach is guarded on the generation.** The session route reads the generation with the row (a wedding with no row reads 0) and passes it to `attachStripeAccount`, whose conflict update writes only while the row still holds it. The INSERT arm needs no guard: it runs only when no row exists, which is generation 0. Without the guard a slow request could fill the id with an account another owner made and has since retired: owner A reads generation N; owner B connects account X under key N and retires it; A's create, still keyed N, gets X back from Stripe inside 24 hours, and A would be recorded as the holder of B's verified account. With it, A's attach writes nothing. The route already answers a write that wrote nothing with 403 `forbidden` (`registry-stripe.ts:278-290`); it now pays one read to tell the two causes apart and answers 409 `stripe_account_changed` when the generation moved, logging `stripe account created but not stored` with the account id either way. The generation is read before the write, never from a `RETURNING`, which does not show what an `AFTER` trigger changed. The reuse and the OAuth attach need no guard: neither uses the key, and each proves control of its account another way (`registry.ts:1565-1630`; Stripe's sign-in).

`cire/api/src/db/setup.ts` mirrors every column, index, table and trigger (`cire/api/tests/db/ddl-lockstep.test.ts` compares triggers too).

## Behaviour

1. **Gift events act on the account the gift was paid into.** `contributionsOnAccount` (`registry.ts:1354`) matches `registry_contributions.stripe_account_id` with the event's account; a row with NULL there falls back to today's join on the settings row. The Standard-account rule in settle and fail (`registry.ts:3177-3190`, `:3304-3315`) takes the type from the gift row the same way. So a refund, a dispute, or a late settle of a delayed bank debit on a retired account reaches its gift, for as long as the account stays connected. A forged event from another account still matches nothing.
2. **Whether the holder is absent** is one predicate, used by the guest gate, every settings record and the hosts flag (behaviour 9). The owner's settings and the refresh's answer (`StripeStatusDto`, `registry.ts:361-366`, `:1136-1145`; the refresh is owner-only) carry it as `payoutHolderAbsent: boolean`, apart from the reason, because the reason `payments_unavailable` comes first on a deployment that cannot take money (`moneyGiftsBlock`, `registry.ts:745-750`), and the panel must still say the account's holder is not an owner.
3. **Money gifts close while the holder is absent.** `accountMoneyBlock` (`registry.ts:720-730`) gains `payout_holder_absent`. The order becomes: `payments_unavailable` (the deployment); then, with no account, `stripe_not_ready`; with an account, `payout_holder_absent` first, before `stripe_not_ready` for an account that cannot charge yet, because only a new account fixes it and the other owners cannot finish someone else's onboarding; then the two currency reasons; then `cash_gifts_off`. `takesMoney`, `moneyIsOpen`, the guest list, the fund pictures and the contribute route (`contributionContext`, `registry.ts:2811`) all follow from that one function, as today. The account is kept: if the holder is seated as an owner again, money gifts open again while the switch is on.
4. **The switch.** `PUT /registry/settings` refuses to turn money gifts on while the holder is absent: 409 `payout_holder_absent`, carrying the settings as that reader may see them, as the other three refusals do (`moneyOnRefusal`, `registry.ts:1030`). Turning them off is always allowed. The switch is the couple's intent, and nothing but that save writes it (`wiki/cire/cire-registry.md`, §Connecting the account, "Intent and capability"), so it survives a change of account as it survives a deauthorization today (decision 7): a newly connected account takes gifts as soon as it can charge, if the switch is on.
5. **What the settings carry** (decision 12). `payoutHolderAbsent` and the reason `payout_holder_absent` go to owners only, through `settingsForReader` (`registry.ts:782-800`), as the connector id does today. To an editor or a viewer, a wedding whose holder is absent reads as an account that cannot take payments, a state they already see in other cases: `payoutHolderAbsent: null`, `stripeChargesEnabled: false`, and, where the reason is `payout_holder_absent`, `stripe_not_ready` in its place; a reason that comes first, such as `payments_unavailable`, passes through unchanged, and a viewer's reason then maps as it does today. An editor's `PUT /registry/settings` turning money on is refused with the same 409 code, `stripe_not_ready`, never `payout_holder_absent`: the route copies the service's code into the body (`routes/registry.ts:333-341`) and editors reach it (`weddingEditor`, `:312`), so the code is chosen by the reader too. Nothing an editor receives tells this case apart from an account still being set up.
6. **Retire.** `POST /api/organiser/weddings/:weddingId/registry/stripe/retire`, body `{ handTo: string | null }`, behind `weddingOwner` and the per-user limiter, in a group of its own without the Gold gate the other `/stripe/*` routes carry (`registry-stripe.ts:217-223`), since it calls Stripe for nothing (§Who may). The route reads the row (its account and generation), then runs one D1 batch of two statements, in this order, each carrying every rule in its `WHERE`:
   - the row still names the account the route read;
   - the caller still owns the wedding and it is live (`ownsLiveWedding`, `registry.ts:1600`);
   - `handTo`, when given, holds an owner seat and is not the caller, and is given only by the holder.

   First an `INSERT … SELECT` writes the `registry_retired_accounts` row, with its reason (`handed_over` or `disconnected` when the holder retires; `replaced` when another owner retires a seated holder's account; `holder_absent` when the holder is absent); then the `UPDATE` sets the id, holder, type, connect time, both flags and the settlement currency to NULL or false. The order matters: once the `UPDATE` has run the row no longer names the account, so an `INSERT` after it would match nothing. A D1 batch is one transaction, so both land or neither. The trigger bumps the generation. The switch is left alone. Cire calls Stripe for nothing. A refusal pays one read to choose its answer: 409 `handover_not_owner`, or 409 `stripe_account_changed` with the settings as they stand (no account, or another one: two owners replacing at once, or a deauthorization in between). Logged `stripe account retired` with the wedding, `by: holder | other_owner | absent_holder` and `handed: yes | no`; refusals `stripe account retire refused` with the reason. No profile id or account id in a log line or a metric; the retired-accounts row is the record of the account.
7. **Old pages and old keys** (decision 8). The reuse lookup (`findReusableContribution`) also matches the wedding's current account, so a page on an old account is never handed back to a guest, and the gift checkout key gains the account id. A guest who already holds a page on the old account can still pay on it until it closes, at most an hour after it was made (`GIFT_CHECKOUT_LIFETIME_SECONDS`, `registry-guest.ts:286`); that gift is paid into the old account and recorded (behaviour 1). Cire does not expire those pages.
8. **Retire notice.** Every retire mails every owner: who retired the account, whom they handed money gifts to if anyone, that guests cannot give money until an owner connects an account, and a link to Money gifts; the named owner's copy speaks to them. A replace while the holder is seated reaches the holder too, since they are an owner; a replace of an absent holder's account reaches every owner, so an owner who did onboard an account nobody recorded hears of it. A departed holder is not mailed (decision 13). A new notice kind beside the three in `owner-notice-email.ts:1-42`. It spends a budget of its own per wedding (a `retire:` key beside the `wedding:` and `actor:` keys, `:278-284`), so a loop of owner changes, which spends the wedding's ordinary budget, cannot use it up before a replace; a loop of retires uses up only its own, and each retire needs an account connected again first. The panel, not the email, stays the one channel that always tells.
9. **The holder cannot leave, be removed or be demoted while holding the account** (decision 26). The three hosts writes refuse it in their own guarded statements, as they refuse removing the last owner (`hostsService.remove`, `cire/api/src/services/hosts.ts:982-994`; `setRole`, `:772-789`):
   - **Which writes.** `DELETE /hosts/:osnProfileId` (`organiser-hosts.ts:674`), `DELETE /hosts/me` (`:743`), and `PUT /hosts/:osnProfileId/role` when it moves the seat from `owner` to any other role (`:473`), the holder's own step-down included. The guard applies only to a seat whose role is `owner`: each `WHERE` gains `(role <> 'owner' OR NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM registry_settings WHERE wedding_id = :wedding AND stripe_account_id IS NOT NULL AND stripe_connected_by_osn_profile_id = :subject))`. A holder who already sits below owner — one who left or was demoted before this ships, as row 3's Kit does — is an absent holder, can leave or be removed, and their account is replaced by any owner. `remove`'s batch first deletes the person's `host_rsvp_notices` rows guarded only on the last-owner rule (`hosts.ts:958-972`); that statement gains the same guard, so a refused removal keeps them, as the method promises. Both statements and the error read sit in one batch, so there is no read-then-write: a retire committed before the batch clears the way, and one after it is too late. A promotion, re-saving `owner`, a change between roles below owner, and the removal of anyone else are untouched.
   - **The refusal.** Each write's batch already reads the seat beside the write to choose its answer when the write matched nothing: `setRole` tells a missing seat from the last-owner guard and then the people limit (`hosts.ts:806-821`), and `remove` reads a remaining seat as the last-owner guard (`:1005-1008`). That read gains the owner count and the same holder sub-select, and the choice becomes: for `setRole`, no seat is 404 `host_not_found` (for `remove`, no seat stays success, as today: it is idempotent, and the routes signal and notify only when a seat went); the wedding's last owner is 409 `last_owner`, which comes first; the holder is a new 409 `holds_payout_account`; and only then, for `setRole`, the people limit. Without the change `remove` would answer every refused holder with `last_owner`. The new failure is caught in both removal handlers and in `roleChangeFailure` (`organiser-hosts.ts:100-111`), or it would end as a 500. Logged as the hosts refusals are (`logRefusal`, `hosts.ts:820`), with the reason `holds_payout_account`, and counted in the existing metrics with that label (`metricHostRemoved`, `metricHostRoleChanged`).
   - **The portal.** `GET /hosts` marks the holder's seat `payoutHolder: true` while that seat's role is `owner`, for an owner reading it, and for no one else, so the portal disables exactly what the API refuses. The hosts panel shows the remove and demote controls on that seat, and the holder's own leave and step-down, as `aria-disabled` with a note beside them, so a keyboard or screen-reader user can still reach the reason (the pattern `RegistrySettingsView.tsx` already uses for the connector note). To another owner: "Sam connected the Stripe account money gifts are paid into. Before Sam can leave or stop being an owner, the account has to be handed over: Sam can hand money gifts to another owner or disconnect it, or you can replace it with your own account in Money gifts." To the holder: "You connected the Stripe account money gifts are paid into. Hand money gifts to another owner or disconnect your account in Money gifts before you leave or step down." Both link Money gifts. A 409 `holds_payout_account` from a page loaded before the account was connected shows the same sentence as a toast and patches the seat's flag.
   - **How the holder hands over.** In Money gifts: hand over (naming an owner) or disconnect, behaviour 6; gifts stay closed from the retire until the next account can take payments (decision 4); a retired Standard account stays connected 120 days (decision 6). Then the leave, removal or demotion goes through, and the owner-change notice goes out as today, with nothing about Stripe in it.
   - **On every tier.** Since the retire carries no Gold gate (§Who may), a holder on a wedding below Gold can hand over, and the refusal needs no exception.
   - **The same rule elsewhere.** The planned "Remove my Cire data" route (account-deletion design, §5, `DELETE /api/organiser/account`, not built) removes every seat of the person at once. When it is built it must refuse while the person holds a payout account on a live Gold wedding with another owner, as it refuses while they alone own a live wedding. Account deletion in Musubi stays the one exception (decision 26): behaviour 10.
10. **Account erasure, the one way a holder vanishes** (decisions 9, 24, 26). The erasure clears the connector (`account-erasure.ts:397-400`), so the holder becomes absent and money gifts close.
    - **The panel** says why to every owner who opens it.
    - **The deletion preview**, which the person sees in Musubi before they confirm, gains one action per live wedding where a profile of the account holds the current payout account and another owner remains. It has one choice, `close`, as its default, so it never blocks the deletion. It says guests will not be able to give money there until another owner connects an account, and, for an Express account cire created, that there is a Stripe account in their name to take care of, reached from the Express Dashboard sign-in, `connect.stripe.com/express_login`. It links Money gifts, so the holder can hand over first. One more read in the preview; at most 20 such actions, then one that stands for the rest, so with the sole-owner actions (at most 100, `account-erasure.ts:82`, `:200`) an answer stays under osn-api's 200. The preview's early answer for more than 100 sole-owner weddings (`account-erasure.ts:200`) appends the payout actions too.
    - **An email to the remaining owners.** The per-wedding batch (`eraseOwnedWedding`, `account-erasure.ts:248-304`) adds to its seat `DELETE`'s `RETURNING` a sub-select of whether the settings row names an account held by one of the account's profiles. When that batch removed the holder's owner seat and another owner remains, cire mails every remaining owner once: an owner who connected the Stripe account has deleted their account; money gifts have stopped; connect your own account to take gifts again, or turn money gifts off (decision 7); a link to Money gifts. It names no one: the person's identity is being erased, and cire keeps nothing about them (account-deletion design, decision 2). It is dispatched as each wedding's batch commits, not at the end of the call, since a later failure of the account-wide clean-up batch fails the call (`account-erasure.ts:525-531`) and a repeat removes no seat, so a notice left to the end would be lost; a repeated call sends nothing again. It uses the owner-notice machinery, which exists in `app.ts:1020-1029`; the build passes it to the internal route's factory, which today takes only `{ osnIssuer, organiserOrigin }` (`app.ts:1166-1169`, `routes/internal-account-deletion.ts:74-77`). `waitUntil` is set for every request (`index.ts:648`), so it runs after the response as owner notices do (`dispatchNotice`, `owner-notice-email.ts:458-464`). It spends the wedding's budget only, since the erasure has no acting owner, which needs a branch of its own in `deliver`'s keys (`owner-notice-email.ts:278-284`). A call erases at most 3 weddings (`MAX_OWNED_WEDDINGS_PER_CALL`, `account-erasure.ts:79`), so it sends at most 3 notices, each one owners read and two budget writes (`owner-notice-email.ts:155-178`, `:190-211`): the call makes at most 1 + 3 × 3 + 21 + 3 × 3 = 40 of the 50 D1 queries a Worker invocation may make, and 6 subrequests (an address lookup and a Resend call per notice).
    - **Not the erased person** (decision 24). osn-api answers no address for an account from the moment its deletion is scheduled until the hard delete, after which there is none (`account-emails.ts:66-73`). The preview line above is the one place cire tells them, before they confirm.
11. **Handing a Standard account back.** A retired Standard account stays connected for 120 days after its retire (decision 6), so late refunds and most disputes on its gifts still reach the log. Then cire hands it back with `POST /oauth/deauthorize`:
    - **Who does it.** A new daily sweep beside the purge's hand-back (`maintenance-sweeps.ts:241-275`), the one new cron job this design adds. At most 3 accounts a run: 1 read and, per account, a release batch, a re-check and a clear-after, so 10 D1 queries and 3 Stripe calls. Every job of the existing 04:00 run shares one invocation's 50 queries, and a busy day can already pass them (`wiki/shared/free-tier-limits.md`, "The daily cire cron shares one invocation"). So it runs on a second cron trigger, where it is alone, and `scheduled` tells the two apart by `event.cron`. The builder checks the account's current cron-trigger allowance at Cloudflare first, since `wiki/shared/free-tier-limits.md` gives no figure for it, and records it there; without one more trigger, it joins the 04:00 run with a cap of 1 account: 4 queries and 1 Stripe call.
    - **When.** It judges the account, not one row, and only its unreleased retired rows: at least one exists and every one is more than 120 days old; no settings row names the account; and no gift on it is `pending` with a session and younger than 14 days, the bound the purge already puts on the same question (`PURGE_PENDING_HOLD_S`, `maintenance-sweeps.ts:88`, `:102`), since a pending row whose Worker died, or whose expiry event was missed, can sit for a year (`registry.ts:3099-3103`). A success releases every row naming the account, so an account released, connected again and retired again is judged afresh.
    - **A refused or failed call.** An error from `/oauth/deauthorize` is not taken as proof the account is gone, since the transport keeps only the error code (`stripe.ts:398-401`) and a misconfigured client id must not mark every account released. The sweep then asks `GET /v1/accounts/:id`: a refusal there means cire no longer reaches the account, and the rows are released. Anything else sets `last_hand_back_at` and logs the account id for a hand fix, as the purge's does. Candidates are taken oldest attempt first, so a few failing accounts never hold the others back.
    - **A connect in between.** If an owner connected the same account while the call was in flight, the sweep clears that row afterwards and logs it, as the OAuth hand-back does (`registry-stripe-oauth.ts:228-232`). A connect whose attach lands after that check can still leave a wedding naming an account cire no longer reaches, as the OAuth hand-back and the purge can today (`registry-stripe-oauth.ts:225-232`, `maintenance-sweeps.ts:265-273`); the panel then shows an account that cannot take payments, and any owner's replace clears it.
    - **Stripe's deauthorization** of an account (`detachStripeAccount`) also releases the retired rows naming it that were connected at or before the event, by the same time guard it applies to settings rows (`registry.ts:2771-2776`), so a late event for an older connection leaves a newer one alone.
    - **The two hand-back paths that exist** count an unreleased retired row as naming the account: the OAuth callback's (`stripeAccountNamed`, `registry.ts:2658-2671`) and the purge's (`maintenance-sweeps.ts:250-263`). Neither then cuts a retired account's 120 days short. The purge of a wedding reads that wedding's retired Standard accounts inside its delete batch, as it reads the settings row (`maintenance-sweeps.ts:179-185`), and hands back those nothing else holds only once the delete has matched (`:200-216`), so a wedding restored meanwhile keeps them.
    - **The unmatched-event log** (`stripeAccountTypeNamed`) reads the type from a retired row too, so a retired Standard account's own shop sales log at info level, not as missing gifts.
12. **The gift log** marks a gift `paidIntoEarlierAccount` when its account is recorded and differs from the account the wedding names now. A row with no recorded account is never marked.

### What a retire does not touch

- **Other weddings naming the same account.** A retire writes only its own wedding's row. Another wedding the holder gave the account to (`adoptStripeAccount`) keeps it, and keeps taking gifts while its own holder is seated there. The refusal in behaviour 9 is per wedding too: holding an account on one wedding does not stop the holder leaving another.
- **The reuse rules** (`controlsSourceAccount`, `ownersSitBesideSource`, `accountMayJoin`, `registry.ts:1565-1630`) are unchanged. A retired account is on no settings row of this wedding, so it is offered to it again only from another wedding the caller still holds it on.
- **`account.updated`** writes every row naming the account (`applyStripeAccountState`, `registry.ts:2454`). A retired account names none on this wedding.

## Planner (#2018)

This note does not design the planner. It must leave room for a planner organisation to hold a wedding's payout account, chosen by the couple per wedding. So the build:

- writes the holder rule once, as the one predicate of behaviour 2 ("is this account's holder in good standing on this wedding"). #2018 adds a second kind of holder, an organisation attached to the wedding with the couple's recorded choice, to that predicate, not a second gate;
- keeps who may retire, and the refusal of behaviour 9, in one place each. #2018 must say how they apply to a planner holder, who holds no owner seat: whether a planner holding the account may be detached before it is handed over, and how the couple move off the planner's account;
- names the reason `payout_holder_absent` and the field `payoutHolderAbsent`, never anything that says owner or connector, so a planner taken off the wedding closes gifts with the same reason and words;
- keeps each gift's account on the gift, so one planner account serving many weddings of different couples settles, refunds and disputes each gift on its own;
- keeps the retire per wedding, and its record free of profile ids, so a couple who move off their planner's account leave the planner's other weddings alone;
- leaves the reuse rules as they are (`accountMayJoin` still refuses an account shared with another couple, `registry.ts:1613-1630`); #2018 decides that.

**The planner's tier.** The owner decided for #2018 that a subscribing planner lifts the couple to Crimson, and that when the planner leaves or lapses the couple are prompted to pay to stay on Crimson or lose access. The previous version of this note built a tier hold that #2018 could have reused; decision 25 removed it, since a tier here belongs to the wedding. So #2018's design needs its own mechanism for that prompt and for ending a planner-given tier. This note builds none of it.

## Rejected

- **Moving the Express account to the other partner.** Stripe does not let cire change an Express account's identity after the first account link, and the original holder keeps the sign-in.
- **Keeping gifts flowing to the old account until the new one is ready.** It needs a second account slot on every row, which every account-state writer and the guest gate would have to learn. The owner accepted the gap (decision 4).
- **Expiring the guest's open pages on the old account.** It buys at most an hour, needs a Stripe client in the hosts and erasure routes, and a gift paid on such a page is recorded anyway. The owner accepted the hour (decision 8).
- **Deleting or rejecting a retired Express account.** Deleting needs a zero balance and ends refunds; rejecting stops the old holder's payouts.
- **Recording whom a hand-over named on the wedding.** It would grant nothing, since any owner may connect once the account is retired, and it goes stale when that owner leaves. The notice carries the name.
- **A profile id in the create key.** Stripe asks for no personal identifiers in keys; a generation counter changes as often.
- **Emailing the erased holder.** osn-api answers no address for an account being deleted (`account-emails.ts:66-73`); the deletion preview tells them instead, before they confirm (decision 24).
- **Blocking account deletion while the person holds a payout account.** Decision 26 keeps erasure as the one way an owner can vanish holding an account; the preview warns and does not block.
- **Gating the retire on Gold, and excusing the refusal below Gold.** The retire calls Stripe for nothing, so it needs no tier; without the gate the refusal holds on every tier, as decision 26 says.
- **A tier tied to the owner who paid, its hold, keep purchase, lapse and refund on request.** Decision 25.
- **A release-time count of the weddings pull request 3 closes.** Its safety does not depend on the count, and no release step runs SQL of that kind today (the one production read a release makes is the migration ledger, `shared/infra/src/production-live.ts`).
- **Routing gifts through the platform.** Decision 2.
