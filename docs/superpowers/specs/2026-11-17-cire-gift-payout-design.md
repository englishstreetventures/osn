# How a cire wedding with several owners receives its cash gifts, and keeps its tier — design

Date: 2026-11-17, revised 2026-11-18 with the owner's answers · Status: owner decisions recorded; awaiting the owner's re-approval and the three refund questions at the end · Issue: englishstreetventures/osn#1950 (follows #1336; related to #1248 and #2018)

## Goal

A wedding can have several owners so that both partners hold owner rights (`2026-10-01-cire-multi-owner-design.md`, §Goal), but two things on it belong to one person. Its cash gifts are paid into one Stripe account, which carries one person's identity and bank details; today that person keeps the account when they stop being an owner, nobody else can replace it, and refunds or disputes on gifts paid into an account the wedding no longer names are lost to its gift log. And its tier was paid for by one owner's card. This note says who may connect, change or replace the payout account; what happens when its holder leaves; how the couple hand it from one partner to the other; what happens to the tier when the owner who paid for it leaves; and what each owner sees. It replaces the stopgap the multi-owner design's decision 14 asked for, which #1336 built.

This note designs; it builds nothing. The owner approves it before any code.

## Decisions

### Owner, 2026-11-17

1. **When the owner who connected the wedding's Stripe account stops being an owner** — removed, left, demoted, or their account erased — all three of these hold:
   - (a) money gifts close while the connector holds no owner seat. The same holds for a wedding whose connector is not recorded. The Money gifts panel says why in plain words.
   - (b) the remaining owners are told, in the owner-change notice and in the Money gifts panel, and pointed at the money-gifts switch.
   - (c) the connector can hand the account to another owner before leaving.

   This replaces two defaults the merged #1975 set: an account with no recorded connector belonged to no owner, and a connector who left kept the account. A third default stays: refreshing the account's status is open to every owner.
2. **Cire never holds gift money.** Gifts stay direct charges on the couple's connected account (`wiki/cire/cire-registry.md`; the `registry_settings.stripe_account_id` comment, `cire/db/src/schema.ts:1016-1019`). A design that routes gifts through the platform account is out.

### Owner, 2026-11-18 (answers to the first version's questions)

3. **The design as a whole**: a hand-over is a new account and a retire, never a move.
4. **The gap during a hand-over is accepted**: gifts are closed from the retire until the next account can take payments.
5. **Any owner may replace an absent holder's account.**
6. **A retired Standard account stays connected for 120 days** before cire hands it back.
7. **The notice and the panel offer two actions** — connect your own account, or turn money gifts off — and the switch survives a change of account.
8. **The hour is accepted** in which a guest already holding a payment page can still pay into the old account.
9. **Erasure**: the panel tells the other owners, the deletion preview warns the holder, and an email goes out as well (the owner chose "also send an email"). See behaviour 12 for who can be emailed and when.
10. **Tier purchases are in scope.** The tier is tied to the OSN account that paid for it. When that paying owner stops being an owner, the remaining owners are prompted to pay to keep the tier. If they do not, the tier ends after the period already given, with notice; nothing is taken back. It has a dedicated E2E test. See [Tier purchases](#tier-purchases).
11. **The holder of an Express account cire created gets one email** when they stop being an owner, telling them there is a Stripe account they must take care of. The holder of a Standard account they connected themselves gets none. See behaviour 9.
12. **Editors are not told** that the account's holder is not an owner.
13. **The departed holder is not told** when the remaining owners replace their account.
14. **At Stripe, not in code:** "Issue refunds" and "Manage disputes" stay on in the Express Dashboard settings (owner action, below).

### Owner, 2026-11-18 (answers to the revised version's questions)

15. **A departed holder's own Standard account stays on the old wedding** until another owner replaces it, and while it does, it cannot be connected through OAuth to another wedding of theirs (`accountMayJoin`, `registry.ts:1623-1630`, as today). Their exit is to revoke cire in their own Stripe Dashboard, which clears it from every wedding.
16. **The tier hold lasts 14 days** from the day the payer stops being an owner.
17. **Only the purchase behind the current tier ties it to a person** (the one `tier_granted_by` names). Earlier steps, comps, unlock codes and migrated tiers tie no one; a tier that lapses onto a bought lower tier ties no one after.
18. **The departing payer may ask for a refund** of the purchase behind the tier, even when the remaining owners pay to keep it. See [Refund on request](#refund-on-request).
19. **The payer is the owner profile that started the checkout**, the nearest the records allow to "the account that paid".
20. **"Nothing is taken back"** means every guest, every seat and every module's data stay; access changes.
21. **A payer who left before this ships starts no hold.**
22. **Only the current tier can be kept**: a Crimson wedding falling to Ivory is offered Crimson.
23. **Owners see which co-owner paid** (`tierPayer`, owners only).
24. **The erased person cannot be emailed**; the deletion preview carries the message.

Still open: the three choices [Refund on request](#refund-on-request) raises (§Needs the owner's approval).

**Owner action at Stripe:** keep "Issue refunds" and "Manage disputes" turned on in the Express Dashboard settings, since after a retire the old holder is the one person who can refund or answer a dispute on their gifts there.

## Non-goals

- A planner as the payout holder or the tier's payer (#2018). This note only leaves room for it: [Planner (#2018)](#planner-2018).
- Two payout accounts on one wedding, or gifts split between partners.
- Splitting a tier's price between owners.
- Any change to who may run a gift refund. Cire refunds no gift on its own today, and still will not.

## What Stripe allows

Checked against Stripe's documentation on 2026-11-17, each page fetched that day. Cire pins API version `2025-03-31.basil` (`cire/api/src/services/stripe.ts:37`).

**Cire cannot hand an Express account from one person to another.**

- "After you create an account link for an Express connected account, you can't read or update its KYC information." (docs.stripe.com/connect/express-accounts, §Create an account link). Cire creates the link on the first Connect press (`registry-stripe.ts:321`), so from then on cire cannot change whose identity the account carries.
- The holder edits their own personal details and payout bank in the Express Dashboard, and signs in there with the account's email and a code sent to their phone or email (docs.stripe.com/connect/express-dashboard, §Account settings, §Direct access). So the person who onboarded keeps control of the account whatever cire records.
- Stripe treats a change to a verified tax ID as a transfer of the account and its balances to a new owner, which the account must accept again under the Stripe Services Agreement (docs.stripe.com/connect/update-verified-information, Accounts v1, §Update the account tax identification number). That is a matter between the two people and Stripe, not a step cire can run.

So **a hand-over is a new account**: the next holder connects one of their own, and the wedding stops naming the old one. This note calls that **retiring** the old account.

**A Standard account** (connected through OAuth, `stripe_account_type = 'standard'`, `schema.ts:1040`) is the holder's own Stripe account. Who owns it inside Stripe is theirs to manage; cire treats it as it treats an Express account, except that cire later hands it back (behaviour 13).

**Retiring is not deleting or rejecting.**

- Deleting a live Express account needs every balance at zero (docs.stripe.com/api/accounts/delete), and a deleted account can no longer refund.
- Rejecting is for fraud or a terms breach, and turns off the account's charges and payouts (docs.stripe.com/api/account/reject). That would stop the old holder's own payouts.
- So a retired Express account stays connected to cire, untouched. Its holder still sees it, and still receives its payouts, in the Express Dashboard.

**Refunds and disputes on old gifts keep arriving while the account stays connected.** For direct charges, "the payment, refunds, and disputes exist on the connected account, so their events use the Connected accounts scope", and "each event for a connected account contains a top-level `account` property" (docs.stripe.com/connect/webhooks, Accounts v1). So cire's gift handlers must match them by the account the gift was paid into, not by the account the wedding names now. Once cire hands a Standard account back, its events stop reaching cire.

**Cire stays liable for a retired Express account's losses.** "Your platform is responsible for losses incurred by Express connected accounts" (docs.stripe.com/connect/express-accounts, §Before you begin). A dispute on an old gift debits the old account whether or not the wedding still names it. Retiring changes nothing here.

**Refunds on old gifts.** In the Express Dashboard an account holder can "issue refunds and manage disputes, if you enable these features" (docs.stripe.com/connect/express-dashboard, §Payments). After a retire, the old holder is the one person who can refund a gift paid into their account from that Dashboard.

**Idempotency keys last at least 24 hours.** Stripe saves the first answer to a key and replays it to a repeat, and may prune a key once it is 24 hours old; it also asks for no "personal identifiers" in keys (docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests). The account-create key is `cire-account-<weddingId>` (`stripe.ts:530`), so a Connect press within 24 hours of a retire would get the retired account back (the hazard noted on #1950 for deauthorization). The key must change with each account, and the attach must refuse an account made under an old key; see [Data model](#data-model).

**Not confirmed:** whether Stripe scopes an idempotency key to the connected account named in `Stripe-Account`. The design does not depend on it: the gift checkout key gains the account id (behaviour 7).

## Terms

- **Holder**: the profile in `stripe_connected_by_osn_profile_id` for the account the wedding names now. Today's code calls them the connector.
- **Seated**: the holder holds a seat with `role = 'owner'` on this wedding.
- **Absent**: there is an account and its holder is not seated, or none is recorded.
- **Retire**: the wedding stops naming its account. Cire calls Stripe for nothing when it retires one.
- **Payer** and **tier hold**: see [Tier purchases](#tier-purchases).

## Who may connect, change or replace the account

| Action | Who | Today |
|---|---|---|
| Connect a wedding with no account (a new Express account, a reuse from another wedding, or OAuth) | any owner | unchanged |
| Continue onboarding | the holder, while seated | unchanged |
| Open the Stripe Dashboard (Standard) | shown to the holder; it is a plain link, and Stripe's own sign-in guards it | unchanged |
| Check again (refresh the status) | any owner | unchanged (the #1975 default that stays) |
| **Hand over**: retire the account and name the owner who should connect the next | the holder, while seated | new |
| **Disconnect**: retire the account, naming no one | the holder, while seated | new |
| **Replace an absent holder's account**: retire it, then connect as above | any owner, while the holder is absent (decision 5) | new |
| Anything above | editors and viewers: nothing | unchanged |

While the holder is seated, no other owner can retire their account. An owner who wants to can remove or demote the holder first, as decision 3 of the multi-owner design allows; the holder then gets the owner-change notice, so it is never silent.

Naming an owner in a hand-over grants them nothing the others lack: once the account is retired any owner may connect the next one, as for any wedding with no account. The name tells the notice whom to address.

Every action above that reaches Stripe or the account sits behind `weddingTier(db, "gold")` (`registry-stripe.ts:220`), so below Gold the retire answers 402 like the rest. Guests cannot reach the gift list below Gold either (`wiki/cire/cire-registry.md`, the note under §Gift registry), so no money moves meanwhile.

Whether the holder is seated is never stored. Every place that builds a settings record works it out with one `EXISTS` on `wedding_hosts`, answered by the unique index `wedding_hosts_wedding_profile_uniq` (`schema.ts:209`): `settingsOnly`, `settingsWithCurrency`, the guest gate's read, `updateSettings`'s `RETURNING` (`registry.ts:2029-2034`), `writeStripeAccountState`'s (`:1116-1121`), the attach's (`:2229-2232`), the reuse's and its refusal read (`:2384-2389`, `:2398-2422`), and `defaultSettings` (no account, so never absent). One missed and the refresh, the panel and the gate disagree.

**The account-create key** becomes `cire-account-<weddingId>-<generation>`, and `createAccount` takes the generation (`stripe.ts:507`). Two owners who press Connect at once still get one account (the race the session route handles at `registry-stripe.ts:288-301`), and the key holds no personal identifier. An account created under the old key format in the 24 hours before the deploy and never stored is orphaned, as such an account is today after 24 hours.

**The attach is guarded on the generation.** The session route reads the generation with the row (a wedding with no row reads 0) and passes it to `attachStripeAccount`, whose conflict update writes only while the row still holds it. The INSERT arm needs no guard: it runs only when no row exists, which is generation 0. Without the guard a slow request could fill the id with an account another owner made and has since retired: owner A reads generation N; owner B connects account X under key N and retires it; A's create, still keyed N, gets X back from Stripe inside 24 hours, and A would be recorded as the holder of B's verified account. With it, A's attach writes nothing. The route already answers a write that wrote nothing with 403 `forbidden` (`registry-stripe.ts:278-290`); it now pays one read to tell the two causes apart and answers 409 `stripe_account_changed` when the generation moved, logging `stripe account created but not stored` with the account id either way. The generation is read before the write, never from a `RETURNING`, which does not show what an `AFTER` trigger changed. The reuse and the OAuth attach need no guard: neither uses the key, and each proves control of its account another way (`registry.ts:1565-1630`; Stripe's sign-in).

`cire/api/src/db/setup.ts` mirrors every column, index, table and trigger (`cire/api/tests/db/ddl-lockstep.test.ts` compares triggers too).

## Behaviour

1. **Gift events act on the account the gift was paid into.** `contributionsOnAccount` (`registry.ts:1354`) matches `registry_contributions.stripe_account_id` with the event's account; a row with NULL there falls back to today's join on the settings row. The Standard-account rule in settle and fail (`registry.ts:3177-3190`, `:3304-3315`) takes the type from the gift row the same way. So a refund, a dispute, or a late settle of a delayed bank debit on a retired account reaches its gift, for as long as the account stays connected. A forged event from another account still matches nothing.
2. **Whether the holder is absent** is one predicate, used by the guest gate, every settings record, the retire and the hosts flag (behaviour 11). The owner's settings and the refresh's answer (`StripeStatusDto`, `registry.ts:361-366`, `:1136-1145`; the refresh is owner-only) carry it as `payoutHolderAbsent: boolean`, apart from the reason, because the reason `payments_unavailable` comes first on a deployment that cannot take money (`moneyGiftsBlock`, `registry.ts:745-750`), and the panel must still say the account's holder is not an owner.
3. **Money gifts close while the holder is absent.** `accountMoneyBlock` (`registry.ts:720-730`) gains `payout_holder_absent`. The order becomes: `payments_unavailable` (the deployment); then, with no account, `stripe_not_ready`; with an account, `payout_holder_absent` first, before `stripe_not_ready` for an account that cannot charge yet, because only a new account fixes it and the other owners cannot finish someone else's onboarding; then the two currency reasons; then `cash_gifts_off`. `takesMoney`, `moneyIsOpen`, the guest list, the fund pictures and the contribute route (`contributionContext`, `registry.ts:2811`) all follow from that one function, as today. The account is kept: if the holder is seated as an owner again, money gifts open again while the switch is on.
4. **The switch.** `PUT /registry/settings` refuses to turn money gifts on while the holder is absent: 409 `payout_holder_absent`, carrying the settings as that reader may see them, as the other three refusals do (`moneyOnRefusal`, `registry.ts:1030`). Turning them off is always allowed. The switch is the couple's intent, and nothing but that save writes it (`wiki/cire/cire-registry.md`, §Connecting the account, "Intent and capability"), so it survives a change of account as it survives a deauthorization today (decision 7): a newly connected account takes gifts as soon as it can charge, if the switch is on.
5. **What the settings carry** (decision 12). `payoutHolderAbsent` and the reason `payout_holder_absent` go to owners only, through `settingsForReader` (`registry.ts:782-800`), as the connector id does today. To an editor or a viewer, a wedding whose holder is absent reads as an account that cannot take payments, a state they already see in other cases: `payoutHolderAbsent: null`, `stripeChargesEnabled: false`, and, where the reason is `payout_holder_absent`, `stripe_not_ready` in its place; a reason that comes first, such as `payments_unavailable`, passes through unchanged, and a viewer's reason then maps as it does today. An editor's `PUT /registry/settings` turning money on is refused with the same 409 code, `stripe_not_ready`, never `payout_holder_absent`: the route copies the service's code into the body (`routes/registry.ts:333-341`) and editors reach it (`weddingEditor`, `:312`), so the code is chosen by the reader too. Nothing an editor receives tells this case apart from an account still being set up.
6. **Retire.** `POST /api/organiser/weddings/:weddingId/registry/stripe/retire`, body `{ handTo: string | null }`, behind `weddingOwner`, Gold and the per-user limiter, like the other `/stripe/*` routes (`registry-stripe.ts:217-223`). The route reads the row (its account and generation), then runs one D1 batch of two statements, in this order, each carrying every rule in its `WHERE`:
   - the row still names the account the route read;
   - the caller still owns the wedding and it is live (`ownsLiveWedding`, `registry.ts:1600`);
   - the caller is the holder, or the holder is absent, read in the statement, so a holder seated again meanwhile is not retired;
   - `handTo`, when given, holds an owner seat and is not the caller.

   First an `INSERT … SELECT` writes the `registry_retired_accounts` row; then the `UPDATE` sets the id, holder, type, connect time, both flags and the settlement currency to NULL or false. The order matters: once the `UPDATE` has run the row no longer names the account, so an `INSERT` after it would match nothing. A D1 batch is one transaction, so both land or neither. The trigger bumps the generation. The switch is left alone. Cire calls Stripe for nothing. A refusal pays one read to choose its answer: 403 `stripe_connector_only` (the holder is seated and is not the caller), 409 `handover_not_owner`, or 409 `stripe_account_changed` with the settings as they stand (no account, or another one: two owners replacing at once, or a deauthorization in between). Logged `stripe account retired` with the wedding, `by: holder | absent_holder` and `handed: yes | no`; refusals `stripe account retire refused` with the reason. No profile id or account id in a log line or a metric; the retired-accounts row is the record of the account.
7. **Old pages and old keys** (decision 8). The reuse lookup (`findReusableContribution`) also matches the wedding's current account, so a page on an old account is never handed back to a guest, and the gift checkout key gains the account id. A guest who already holds a page on the old account can still pay on it until it closes, at most an hour after it was made (`GIFT_CHECKOUT_LIFETIME_SECONDS`, `registry-guest.ts:286`); that gift is paid into the old account and recorded (behaviour 1). Cire does not expire those pages.
8. **Retire notice.** Every retire mails every owner: who retired the account, whom they handed money gifts to if anyone, that guests cannot give money until an owner connects an account, and a link to Money gifts; the named owner's copy speaks to them. A retire of an absent holder's account mails every owner too, so an owner who did onboard an account nobody recorded hears of it. The departed holder is not mailed (decision 13). A new notice kind beside the three in `owner-notice-email.ts:1-42`, spending the same per-wedding and per-actor budget. A notice can be throttled by that budget (`:33-41`, `:278-294`), so the panel, not the email, is the one channel that always tells.
9. **When the holder stops being an owner** — removed, demoted or leaving (erasure is behaviour 12). Whether the person held the wedding's current account, and whether they paid for its tier ([Tier purchases](#tier-purchases)), is decided by the hosts write itself: the guarded `DELETE` or `UPDATE` in `hostsService` (`remove`, `hosts.ts:982-994`; `setRole`, `:772-789`) adds to its `RETURNING` sub-selects of whether the settings row names an account held by that profile, of that account's type, and of whether the wedding's tier rests on a purchase of theirs. That costs no statement and cannot be beaten by an owner who retires the account a moment later. Each consequence also needs the change to take an owner below owner (`previousRole` was `owner` and the new role is not, `hosts.ts:840`), since the flags alone are also true when someone already demoted is promoted again.
   - **The other owners** get one paragraph in the owner-change notice (`owner-notice-email.ts:323-362`): money gifts have stopped, because gifts would still be paid to that person; connect your own account to take gifts again, or turn money gifts off so they stay off if that person comes back; a link to Money gifts (decision 7).
   - **The person leaving, when the account is an Express account cire created** (decision 11), gets one email of its own, `payout-account-left`: there is a Stripe account in their name that took this wedding's gifts; gifts already given stay in it, and any refund or dispute on them happens there; how to reach it (the Express Dashboard sign-in, `connect.stripe.com/express_login`) and that they can close it there once it is settled. It is sent once per change, by the write that took them off the owner seat, and only when there is something in the account to look after: it can take charges, or a gift was paid into it. A holder who pressed Connect and never finished onboarding gets none. It is not sent while the same account still takes gifts for another live wedding where they hold an owner seat, since there they still look after it. It goes whether or not the owner-change notice mails them. The fresh-seat rule (`FRESH_SEAT_MS`, `owner-notice-email.ts:113-120`) stops an owner sending mail to a stranger by seating and removing them; it is safe to pass by here because a holder is recorded only by their own Connect press or OAuth sign-in (the attach records its caller, `registry.ts:2176`, `:2204`), so the recipient chose to be one. It spends the acting owner's daily budget (`owner-notice-email.ts:33-41`), as the owner-change notice from the same change does, so an owner demoting and promoting the holder in a loop runs out after 30 emails that day. Like every owner notice it can also fail quietly, logged and counted, when osn-api does not answer or holds no address (`owner-notice-email.ts:28-31`, `:260-262`). Their owner-change copy, when sent, says nothing about Stripe, so the account is the subject of one email. When they paid for the tier, that copy says they may ask for a refund until the window's last day, and links "Your purchases" ([Refund on request](#refund-on-request)).
   - **The holder of a Standard account** gets no email about it (decision 11): it is their own Stripe account, run in their own Dashboard.
10. **The gift log** marks a gift `paidIntoEarlierAccount` when its account is recorded and differs from the account the wedding names now. A row with no recorded account is never marked.
11. **Warnings before the holder goes.** `GET /hosts` marks the holder's seat `payoutHolder: true`, for an owner reading it, and for no one else. The hosts panel warns before an owner removes or demotes the holder, and before the holder leaves or steps down, that money gifts stop until another owner connects their own account, and offers the hand-over first. The tier work adds the same for the payer (`tierPayer: true`; the tier falls on a date unless an owner pays to keep it), in pull requests 7 and 9, so no warning promises a hold before holds exist.
12. **Account erasure** (decision 9). The erasure already clears the connector (`account-erasure.ts:397-400`), so the holder becomes absent and money gifts close.
    - **The panel** says why to every owner who opens it.
    - **The deletion preview**, which the person sees in Musubi before they confirm, gains one action per live wedding where a profile of the account holds the current payout account, and another owner remains. It has one choice, `close`, as its default, so it never blocks the deletion. It says guests will not be able to give money there, and, for an Express account cire created, that there is a Stripe account in their name to take care of. It links Money gifts, so the holder can hand over first. Pull request 7 adds the payer: the same action, or one of its own where they paid for the tier and do not hold the account, saying the remaining owners will be asked to pay to keep it, and that to ask for a refund of the tier they must leave the wedding before deleting their account. Both kinds share the cap of 20 below. One read before the batches; at most 20 such actions, then one that stands for the rest, so with the sole-owner actions (at most 100, `account-erasure.ts:82`, `:200`) an answer stays under osn-api's 200.
    - **An email to the remaining owners.** The per-wedding batch (`eraseOwnedWedding`, `account-erasure.ts:248-304`) adds to its seat `DELETE`'s `RETURNING` the same sub-selects as behaviour 9. When that batch removed an owner seat of the holder, and another owner remains, cire mails every remaining owner once: an owner who connected the Stripe account has deleted their account; money gifts have stopped; the two actions. Pull request 7 adds the payer and the tier's hold and date to the same notice, read from the same `RETURNING`, so it adds no statement. It names no one: the person's identity is being erased, and cire keeps nothing about them (account-deletion design, decision 2). It is dispatched as each wedding's batch commits, not at the end of the call, since a later failure of the account-wide clean-up batch fails the call (`account-erasure.ts:525-531`) and a repeat removes no seat, so a notice left to the end would be lost; a repeated call sends nothing again. It uses the owner-notice machinery, which exists in `app.ts:1020-1029`; pull request 5 passes it to the internal route's factory, which today takes only `{ osnIssuer, organiserOrigin }` (`app.ts:1166-1169`, `routes/internal-account-deletion.ts:74-77`). `waitUntil` is set for every request (`index.ts:648`), so it runs after the response as owner notices do (`dispatchNotice`, `owner-notice-email.ts:458-464`). It spends the wedding's budget only, since the erasure has no acting owner. A call erases at most 3 weddings (`MAX_OWNED_WEDDINGS_PER_CALL`, `account-erasure.ts:79`), so it sends at most 3 notices, each one owners read and two budget writes (`owner-notice-email.ts:155-178`, `:190-211`): with one hold write per wedding batch the call makes at most 1 + 4 × 3 + 21 + 3 × 3 = 43 of the 50 D1 queries a Worker invocation may make, and 6 subrequests (an address lookup and a Resend call per notice).
    - **Not the erased person.** osn-api answers no address for an account being deleted (`account-emails.ts:66-73`), from the moment the person asks until the hard delete, after which there is none. So the leaver email of behaviour 9 cannot reach someone who leaves by erasure; the preview line above is the one place cire tells them, before they confirm.
13. **Handing a Standard account back.** A retired Standard account stays connected for 120 days after its retire (decision 6), so late refunds and most disputes on its gifts still reach the log. Then cire hands it back with `POST /oauth/deauthorize`:
    - **Who does it.** A new daily sweep beside the purge's hand-back (`maintenance-sweeps.ts:241-275`), at most 3 accounts a run. It runs on a second cron trigger, with the tier sweep ([Tier purchases](#tier-purchases)), not inside the existing 04:00 run: every job of that run shares one invocation's 50 D1 queries, and a busy day can already pass them (`wiki/shared/free-tier-limits.md`, "The daily cire cron shares one invocation"). `scheduled` tells the two apart by `event.cron`. The second run makes at most 1 read and, per account, a release batch, a re-check and a clear-after: 10 queries and 3 Stripe calls, beside the tier sweep's 17 queries and 6 subrequests below, 27 of 50 and 9 subrequests in all. The builder checks the account's cron-trigger allowance in `wiki/shared/free-tier-limits.md` first; without one more trigger, both sweeps join the 04:00 run with a cap of 1 each.
    - **When.** It judges the account, not one row, and only its unreleased retired rows: at least one exists and every one is more than 120 days old; no settings row names the account; and no gift on it is `pending` with a session and younger than 14 days, the bound the purge already puts on the same question (`PURGE_PENDING_HOLD_S`, `maintenance-sweeps.ts:88`, `:102`), since a pending row whose Worker died, or whose expiry event was missed, can sit for a year (`registry.ts:3099-3103`). A success releases every row naming the account, so an account released, connected again and retired again is judged afresh.
    - **A refused or failed call.** An error from `/oauth/deauthorize` is not taken as proof the account is gone, since the transport keeps only the error code (`stripe.ts:398-401`) and a misconfigured client id must not mark every account released. The sweep then asks `GET /v1/accounts/:id`: a refusal there means cire no longer reaches the account, and the rows are released. Anything else sets `last_hand_back_at` and logs the account id for a hand fix, as the purge's does. Candidates are taken oldest attempt first, so a few failing accounts never hold the others back.
    - **A connect in between.** If an owner connected the same account while the call was in flight, the sweep clears that row afterwards and logs it, as the OAuth hand-back does (`registry-stripe-oauth.ts:228-232`). A connect whose attach lands after that check can still leave a wedding naming an account cire no longer reaches, as the OAuth hand-back and the purge can today (`registry-stripe-oauth.ts:225-232`, `maintenance-sweeps.ts:265-273`); the panel then shows an account that cannot take payments, and its holder's Disconnect clears it.
    - **Stripe's deauthorization** of an account (`detachStripeAccount`) also releases the retired rows naming it that were connected at or before the event, by the same time guard it applies to settings rows (`registry.ts:2771-2776`), so a late event for an older connection leaves a newer one alone.
    - **The two hand-back paths that exist** count an unreleased retired row as naming the account: the OAuth callback's (`stripeAccountNamed`, `registry.ts:2658-2671`) and the purge's (`maintenance-sweeps.ts:250-263`). Neither then cuts a retired account's 120 days short. The purge of a wedding reads that wedding's retired Standard accounts inside its delete batch, as it reads the settings row (`maintenance-sweeps.ts:179-185`), and hands back those nothing else holds only once the delete has matched (`:200-216`), so a wedding restored meanwhile keeps them.
    - **The unmatched-event log** (`stripeAccountTypeNamed`) reads the type from a retired row too, so a retired Standard account's own shop sales log at info level, not as missing gifts.

### What a retire does not touch

- **Other weddings naming the same account.** A retire writes only its own wedding's row. Another wedding the holder gave the account to (`adoptStripeAccount`) keeps it, and keeps taking gifts while its own holder is seated there.
- **The reuse rules** (`controlsSourceAccount`, `ownersSitBesideSource`, `accountMayJoin`, `registry.ts:1565-1630`) are unchanged. A retired account is on no settings row of this wedding, so it is offered to it again only from another wedding the caller still holds it on.
- **`account.updated`** writes every row naming the account (`applyStripeAccountState`, `registry.ts:2454`). A retired account names none on this wedding.

## Tier purchases

Decision 10 brings the tier into this design. What the code does today, and what changes.

### Today

- **A tier is permanent.** A purchase raises `weddings.tier` once and nothing lowers it on its own: the grant is a conditional `UPDATE` that only ever moves up (`tierGrantStatement`, `cire/api/src/services/tiers.ts:257-285`; the column comment, `cire/db/src/schema.ts:110-112`). Only an operator lowers one, with `grant-tier.ts --lower`, which also marks the purchases above the new tier `refunded` (`wiki/cire/cire-entitlements.md`, §Changing a wedding's tier by hand). There is no period, expiry or renewal anywhere on a tier or a purchase.
- **The buyer is recorded and nothing reads it.** `wedding_upgrade_purchases.created_by_osn_profile_id` (`schema.ts:2092-2095`) is written when the purchase starts (`upgrades.ts:1338`) and cleared to `deleted` by an erasure (`account-erasure.ts:471-473`); no other code reads it. `weddings.tier_granted_by = 'stripe:<purchase id>'` names the purchase behind the current tier (`schema.ts:114-126`). So "the tier is tied to the account that paid" is new behaviour.
- **A wedding may reach its tier in steps.** A Crimson bought as an upgrade from Gold is priced for the difference and records `from_tier = 'gold'` (`schema.ts:2043-2046`); its grant applies only while the wedding still holds Gold (`fromTierHeld`, `upgrades.ts:140-145`).
- **Buying the tier a wedding already holds is refused**, 409 `already_held` (`upgrades.ts:1229`), and the catalogue offers only tiers above the wedding's own (`routes/upgrade.ts:116-117`).
- **A paid checkout either grants its tier or is refunded automatically** (`wiki/cire/cire-upgrades.md`, §Taking money and granting nothing).

### What changes

**Who paid.** The tier is tied to the purchase behind it: the one `weddings.tier_granted_by` names (`stripe:<purchase id>`) while `tier_source = 'purchase'`. Its buyer, `created_by_osn_profile_id`, is the **payer**, as a profile (decision 19). Only that purchase counts (decision 17): a step bought earlier, such as Gold before another owner's Crimson-from-Gold upgrade, is no longer tied to its buyer once a later purchase stands on it, and a tier from a comp, an unlock code or the 0073 migration ties no one. Reading one row keeps this exact: a comp or a migrated tier leaves no purchase row behind (`grant-tier.ts:76-79`; `migrations-archive/0073_wedding_tiers.sql:37-38`), so no rule that walked the purchase history could account for them. An erased buyer reads `deleted` (`account-erasure.ts:471-473`), which holds no seat, so an erased payer counts as gone.

**The hold.** When the payer stops being an owner — removed, demoted, leaving (behaviour 9) or erased (behaviour 12) — the same write sets a tier hold, if none stands:

- `tier_hold_until`, when the tier falls: 14 days after the change (decision 16);
- `tier_hold_grant`, the `tier_granted_by` value the hold is about, here `stripe:<purchase id>`;
- `tier_hold_floor`, the tier it falls to: the purchase's `from_tier`, the tier the wedding held when it was bought, or Ivory when that is NULL;
- `tier_hold_reason = 'payer_left'`.

It names no person, so an erasure keeps nothing about the payer. A payer who left before this ships starts nothing: the hold is set only by the write that takes them off their seat.

**The hold follows the grant.** It lowers only while `weddings.tier_granted_by` still equals `tier_hold_grant`. Every other writer of the tier writes a new `tier_granted_by`: an ordinary upgrade (`tierGrantStatement`, `tiers.ts:267-285`), an unlock code (`unlock-codes.ts:144-171`), an operator's `grant-tier.ts` with or without `--lower` (`grant-tier.ts:76-79`), and a keep purchase. Those are every writer of `weddings.tier` in `cire/` besides migration 0073, and each clears the four hold columns in the same `UPDATE` that replaces the grant (`tiers.ts:276`, `unlock-codes.ts:154-159`, `grant-tier.ts:78`), so a hold never outlives its grant. Every reader of a hold (the next hold write's "if none stands", the banner, the catalogue's keep offer, `startPurchase`) also treats it as standing only while `tier_hold_grant = tier_granted_by`, so a row written by a Worker built before this, or by hand, cannot act on a stale hold; the tier sweep clears such holds in one statement on every run, outside its cap. So owners who upgrade during a hold keep what they bought, and a comp or a code that raises the tier stands. A comp of the tier the wedding already holds writes nothing (`grant-tier.ts:90-93`), as an unlock code for it is refused (`unlock-codes.ts:144`); an operator ends a hold by hand with `grant-tier.ts <current tier> --lower`, which sets the tier outright and so replaces the grant, and `wiki/cire/cire-entitlements.md` says so. One hold per wedding is enough, since a hold follows the one grant the tier rests on. A purchase with a legacy entitlement key, or with no `from_tier` (rows written before migration 0073), starts no hold, since its floor cannot be known.

- **The prompt.** The owner-change notice, or the erasure notice, tells the remaining owners, with the date. The wedding read every portal page already makes, the wedding list, carries the hold to every member while it matches its grant: `tierHold: { until, floor }`, or `null`. Every member's portal shows a banner on every page until the hold ends: "This wedding returns to Ivory on 2 December unless an owner pays to keep Gold. Nothing is deleted." Owners see a "Keep Gold" button; editors and viewers see that an owner can keep it. The catalogue, which every member may read (`routes/upgrade.ts:100-101`), lists the keep offer to them all, as it lists prices today.
- **The keep purchase.** For a wedding under a hold, the catalogue offers the move from `tier_hold_floor` to the current tier, at the Price that move already has (`upgrade-catalogue.ts:129-135`: Gold, Crimson, or Crimson from Gold). `startPurchase`, which today refuses a tier the wedding holds (`upgrades.ts:1227-1229`, `already_held`), accepts that one tier while a hold stands, quotes it from the floor rather than from the current tier, and records `from_tier = tier_hold_floor` (today it records the current tier, `upgrades.ts:1321`). Its settle grants in one of two ways, each in the same batch as today's grant: while the wedding holds that tier with `tier_granted_by = tier_hold_grant`, it re-attributes the tier (`tier_source = 'purchase'`, `tier_granted_by` the new purchase) and clears the hold; once the hold has lapsed and the wedding is on the floor, it raises it as any purchase priced from the floor does (`fromTierHeld`, `upgrades.ts:140-145`). Otherwise — another owner kept it first, the payer came back, or something else replaced the grant — the existing refund path refunds it (`refundReason`, `upgrades.ts:131-132`), with a reason of its own in `platform_refunds.reason` (`schema.ts:2172-2181`). Two things the build must hold to. Every grant batch for a keep purchase carries both arms, each guarded in its own SQL, and the purchase's flip to `succeeded` is guarded on either arm holding, since a replay re-grants with no fresh read (`upgrades.ts:1052-1056`) and the sweep can land between the read and the batch; the refund claim's `not(grantable)` (`upgrades.ts:894`) mirrors the same two arms. And `samePurchase` (`upgrades.ts:1269`), which lets a second owner take over an open checkout for the same move, compares a keep's `from_tier` with the floor, not with the current tier. Only the current tier can be kept: a Crimson wedding falling to Ivory is offered Crimson, not Gold; owners who want Gold let it fall and buy Gold.
- **The payer seated again** as an owner before the date clears the hold: `remove` and `setRole`'s `RETURNING` (behaviour 9) and `add`, which can seat an owner too (`hosts.ts:586`), check whether the buyer of the purchase `tier_hold_grant` names is now seated, and whether that purchase still reads `succeeded`. A purchase refunded on request (below; it ends `auto_refunded`, or `refunded` where someone refunded it by hand first) keeps its hold running whoever is seated, so a refund followed by a return never leaves a tier standing on money given back.
- **When the date passes**, the daily sweep lowers the tier, in one batch per wedding guarded on the hold as read and on `tier_granted_by = tier_hold_grant`: the tier becomes `tier_hold_floor`, with `tier_source = 'lapsed'` and `tier_granted_by = 'lapsed:<purchase id>'`; the hold is cleared; and the purchase moves from `succeeded` to a new status, `lapsed`, in a statement guarded on `succeeded` alone: the lowering does not depend on it, so a purchase refunded on request meanwhile (`refunding`, `auto_refunded`, `refunded`) never holds the lowering up. Settle's `after` answers `lapsed` with a case of its own, acknowledging and granting nothing, rather than its catch-all error (`upgrades.ts:1069-1076`), and the portal's purchase poll learns it (`PurchaseState`, `cire/host/src/lib/upgrade-api.ts:63-72`); `tier_source` gains `lapsed` in its Drizzle enum (`schema.ts:125`). The status is what keeps the wedding down: settle grants again on any redelivery of a `succeeded` purchase ("A replay of a granted purchase stays a grant", `upgrades.ts:1052-1056`), and `grantable` admits the purchase `tier_granted_by` names (`upgrades.ts:520-523`), so without it a redelivered `checkout.session.completed`, or one an operator resends, would raise the wedding again. Like `refunded`, `lapsed` never grants; unlike it, it records no refund, since no money moves. The migration adds it to the two status triggers (`cire/api/src/db/setup.ts:793-802`, and the migration that created them). The sweep mails every owner that the wedding is now on the lower tier. At most 3 weddings a run, on the second cron trigger of behaviour 13: 1 read of holds due and 1 clear of stale holds, then per wedding the lowering batch (2 statements), the owners read and the budget batch (2): 17 queries and 6 subrequests.
- **Nothing is taken back** (decision 20). Lowering keeps every guest and every seat over the new limits (`wiki/cire/cire-entitlements.md`, §Guest cap, §People limit, "A wedding over its limit keeps everyone"), and keeps every module's data. What changes is access: modules above the new tier lock; adding guests or people waits until the wedding is back within its limit; a premium invite design falls back for guests below Crimson (`wiki/cire/cire-entitlements.md:101-105`); and below Gold the gift list closes to guests, and money gifts with it.

### Refund on request

Decision 18: the departing payer may ask for a refund of the purchase behind the tier, even when the remaining owners pay to keep it.

**What exists today.** No owner can ask for an upgrade refund. Cire refunds a purchase on its own only when a paid checkout cannot grant its tier: settle claims the row from an open status into `refunding`, in the same batch as its `platform_refunds` record (`upgrades.ts:880-905`; the record, `schema.ts:2150-2200`), then asks Stripe for a full refund under one key per purchase (`refundPurchase`, `upgrades.ts:695`). The daily reconciliation retries a refund that did not land, and every owner gets the `upgrade-refund` email once Stripe accepts it (`wiki/cire/cire-upgrades.md`, §The refund). An operator refunds anything else by hand. So the request is new in three places, and reuses the rest:

- **Which purchase, and until when.** The write that starts a hold (pull request 6) also sets `refund_requestable_until` on the purchase the hold names: the day the payer left plus the window (§Needs the owner's approval, item 1). Nothing else sets it. So only the purchase behind the tier when its buyer left can be refunded on request: not an earlier step under it, not a legacy-key purchase or one with no `from_tier`, which starts no hold, and not one whose buyer left before pull request 6 shipped (decision 21). The value lives on the purchase, so it survives a keep purchase and the sweep, which both clear the hold. A payer who leaves while only the second stack is live has it set, and may ask once the third stack ships, inside the window.
- **A claim from `succeeded` or `lapsed`.** Today's claim moves only open rows. The request's claim moves the payer's own purchase from `succeeded` or `lapsed` to `refunding` and writes its record with a new reason, `payer_requested`, in one batch, guarded in its `WHERE` on the conditions below and on no `platform_refunds` record existing for the purchase: the record's purchase id is unique (`schema.ts:2163`), but its insert does nothing on a conflict (`upgrades.ts:926`), so the guard is the claim's own. From there `refundPurchase`, the reconciliation (which reads records by status, so it picks these up, `upgrades.ts:1549-1562`) and the record work as they do. A refund Stripe accepts ends `auto_refunded` (`upgrades.ts:749-758`), or `refunded` where someone had refunded the charge by hand first (`:777`); both grant nothing on any replay (`:1057-1062`). The record's reason tells a requested refund from one cire could not grant.
- **A route for someone who is no longer an owner.** The payer has no seat that the wedding gates would admit, so two new routes sit behind the organiser session alone (`osnAuth`, `middleware/osn-auth.ts:92-105`), with the per-user limiter (`rateLimitMiddlewareByUser`, `middleware/rate-limit.ts:72-88`): `GET /api/organiser/me/purchases` and `POST /api/organiser/me/purchases/:purchaseId/refund`. No `/me` route exists today. The list answers the caller's purchases that have `refund_requestable_until` set, with their status, so the payer can follow a refund through `refunding` to its end, or see one that failed and is with a person. The request refunds only a purchase whose buyer is the caller's profile, that reads `succeeded` or `lapsed`, whose wedding the caller holds no owner seat on, and whose `refund_requestable_until` has not passed. Every other purchase is one 404, so the route says nothing about purchases that are not the caller's. The wedding need not be live: a refund is a money record, and the request works while the purchase row exists; the purge of a deleted wedding takes the row, and with it the request.
- **A page outside any wedding.** The payer can no longer open the wedding, so the portal's account menu (`cire/host/src/components/ProfileMenu.tsx:83`) gains "Your purchases", listing what the list route returns, each with its wedding, tier, amount and status, and, while it may be refunded, the last day and a refund button behind a confirmation. The payer's own owner-change email, when it is sent (behaviour 9), says they may ask and links the page.

**The amount** is the whole purchase, as every refund cire makes today is: `refundPurchase` refunds the PaymentIntent in full. A part refund would need a rule for what part of a one-off tier has been used, which nothing in the design gives.

**What the tier does.** A refunded purchase grants nothing, so its grant ends:

- **When the remaining owners have already kept the tier**, it rests on their keep purchase, and nothing changes for the wedding.
- **While the hold stands**, the hold keeps its date (§Needs the owner's approval, item 2): the remaining owners were promised 14 days' notice, and a keep purchase still works as designed. On the date the sweep lowers the tier as for any hold; the purchase no longer reads `succeeded`, so the sweep does not mark it `lapsed`.
- **When the remaining owners bought a higher tier on top of it** during the hold (Crimson from Gold, say), that ordinary grant cleared the hold, and the wedding stands on their purchase for the step above and on the payer's for the step below. Refunding the payer leaves the higher tier standing with its lower step given back. §Needs the owner's approval, item 3.
- **After the tier has lapsed**, nothing changes for the wedding: it is already on the floor.

**Who is told.** The remaining owners get an `upgrade-refund` email when Stripe accepts the refund, sent by `refundIssued`, which mails current owners only (`owner-notice-email.ts:404-430`). Its copy today says cire could not upgrade the wedding and has refunded the payment (`shared/email/src/templates/upgrade-refund.ts:40-44`), which is false here, so pull request 10 adds a variant for `payer_requested`, passed through `RefundNotice`: a former owner was refunded the Gold they paid for, and, while a hold stands, the date the tier falls unless an owner keeps it. The payer sees the outcome on the page.

**Who cannot ask.** An erased payer: their profile is gone, and the buyer column reads `deleted`. So the deletion preview warns them: a payer still seated is told, in the wedding's tier line (behaviour 12), to leave the wedding first if they want to ask; and a payer who has already left gets one action per purchase they may still ask about, with its last day, saying deletion ends it (default `close`, inside the same cap of 20). A payer who is an owner again: the hold is theirs to clear, and the purchase is the tier's grant.

### Squaring it with the standing rules

- **"A paid upgrade always grants its tier or is refunded automatically."** That rule is about the moment money is taken, and still holds there. A keep purchase grants (it re-attributes the tier and clears the hold, or raises a lowered wedding) or, when it cannot, the existing settle path refunds it automatically, as for any purchase that cannot grant. The departing payer's purchase did grant its tier, for as long as they were an owner and through the hold. Its lapse is neither a grant nor a refund: no money moves. A refund the departing payer asks for (decision 18) is a second path to a refund, after the grant. The refunded purchase never grants again on any replay. The tier it stood on is not lowered by the refund itself: it falls on the hold's date (§Needs the owner's approval, item 2), or stays where another purchase now carries it, a keep purchase or a higher tier bought on top (item 3). [Refund on request](#refund-on-request) has the cases.
- **"A tier only ever moves up on its own."** This rule changes: a tier now also falls on its own when a hold lapses. The column comment (`schema.ts:110-112`), `tierGrantStatement`'s comment (`tiers.ts:257-261`), the status list's comment (`schema.ts:2047-2057`) and `wiki/cire/cire-entitlements.md` say so in the same pull request.
- **A deleted or erased account's data is erased.** The tie to the payer is the purchase row's buyer column, which the erasure already sets to `deleted`. The hold the erasure starts carries a time, a grant, a tier and a reason, never a person, and its notice names no one. The purchase row and the sale in `platform_sales` stay, as money records do today.

## Planner (#2018)

This note does not design the planner. It must leave room for a planner organisation to hold a wedding's payout account, chosen by the couple per wedding, and for the owner's #2018 decisions on the tier: a subscribing planner lifts the couple to Crimson; when the planner leaves or lapses the couple are prompted to pay to stay on Crimson or lose access; a couple who already paid when a planner attaches choose between a refund and credit for physical invites. So the build:

- **builds the tier hold once.** The hold columns, the banner and its prompt, the keep purchase, the notices and the lowering sweep of [Tier purchases](#tier-purchases) are what #2018 needs when its planner leaves or lapses. The hold follows a grant, not a purchase: #2018's lift to Crimson writes a `tier_granted_by` of its own, and its hold names that grant, the floor the couple held before, and a reason of its own (`planner_left`). #2018 supplies what differs: the event that starts its hold, the date (a lapse arrives at the end of the paid period, so its notice cannot come from the period itself), and what clears it, such as a renewal. It does not build a second prompt or a second sweep;
- writes the holder rule once, as the one predicate of behaviour 2 ("is this account's holder in good standing on this wedding"). #2018 adds a second kind of holder, an organisation attached to the wedding with the couple's recorded choice, to that predicate, not a second gate;
- keeps who may retire in one place too (the holder, or anyone while the holder is not in good standing). #2018 must widen it, since a planner in good standing holds no owner seat and the couple must still be able to move off the planner's account;
- names the reason `payout_holder_absent` and the field `payoutHolderAbsent`, never anything that says owner or connector, so a planner taken off the wedding closes gifts with the same reason and words;
- keeps each gift's account on the gift, so one planner account serving many weddings of different couples settles, refunds and disputes each gift on its own;
- keeps the retire per wedding, and its record free of profile ids, so a couple who move off their planner's account leave the planner's other weddings alone;
- leaves the reuse rules as they are (`accountMayJoin` still refuses an account shared with another couple, `registry.ts:1613-1630`); #2018 decides that.

## Rejected

- **Moving the Express account to the other partner.** Stripe does not let cire change an Express account's identity after the first account link, and the original holder keeps the sign-in.
- **Keeping gifts flowing to the old account until the new one is ready.** It needs a second account slot on every row, which every account-state writer and the guest gate would have to learn. The owner accepted the gap (decision 4).
- **Expiring the guest's open pages on the old account.** It buys at most an hour, needs a Stripe client in the hosts and erasure routes, and a gift paid on such a page is recorded anyway. The owner accepted the hour (decision 8).
- **Deleting or rejecting a retired Express account.** Deleting needs a zero balance and ends refunds; rejecting stops the old holder's payouts.
- **Recording whom a hand-over named on the wedding.** It would grant nothing, since any owner may connect once the account is retired, and it goes stale when that owner leaves. The notice carries the name.
- **A profile id in the create key.** Stripe asks for no personal identifiers in keys; a generation counter changes as often.
- **Emailing the erased leaver.** osn-api answers no address for an account being deleted (`account-emails.ts:66-73`); the deletion preview tells them instead, before they confirm.
- **Reading the tier's period from the code.** There is none: a one-off purchase grants a permanent tier. The owner set the hold at 14 days (decision 16).
- **A part refund on request.** Every refund cire makes is of the whole PaymentIntent, and a one-off tier has no measure of how much of it was used.
- **Working out a "paid floor" from the purchase history.** A comp or a migrated tier leaves no purchase row once a later purchase has overwritten the wedding's attribution, so the history cannot say what the wedding held for free. The hold follows the one grant the tier rests on instead.
- **Marking a lapsed purchase `refunded`, as `grant-tier.ts --lower` does.** No money goes back; a status of its own (`lapsed`) stops a replay from granting it again without saying so.
- **Lowering the tier at once when the payer leaves.** Decision 10 gives the remaining owners notice and the chance to pay.
- **A release-time count of the weddings pull request 3 closes.** Its safety does not depend on the count, and no release step runs SQL of that kind today (the one production read a release makes is the migration ledger, `shared/infra/src/production-live.ts`).
- **Routing gifts through the platform.** Decision 2.

## Needs the owner's approval

The design as revised, recording decisions 3 to 24, and the choices the refund on request raises:

1. **How long the departing payer may ask for a refund**, counted from the day they left and stored on the purchase, so it survives a keep purchase and the sweep. Options: (a) 14 days, ending with the hold's date, so every party knows by then where the tier and the money stand; (b) a longer time, such as 30 days; (c) no limit, until the purchase row goes with a purged wedding. Under (a) a lapsed purchase is past its window the moment it lapses, so the claim from `lapsed` matters only under (b) or (c). Recommendation: (a).
2. **What the tier does when the payer is refunded while the hold stands.** Options: (a) the hold keeps its date, so the remaining owners still get their 14 days' notice, on a tier nobody has paid for meanwhile; (b) the tier falls at once to the floor, since the purchase behind it has been given back, and the remaining owners can buy it again. Recommendation: (a), because decision 10 promises notice before the tier ends.
3. **A refund after the remaining owners bought a higher tier on top.** If they bought Crimson from Gold during the hold, the wedding stands on the payer's Gold for its lower step. Options: (a) allow the refund, and the wedding keeps Crimson having paid only the step above Gold, as decision 17 already lets owners who upgrade during a hold keep the step below; (b) refuse it once a purchase priced from the payer's tier stands on the wedding, and the page says why. Recommendation: (a), since it follows decisions 17 and 18 as given; (b) keeps the payer's money where a tier still rests on it.
